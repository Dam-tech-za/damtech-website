## Facts used in the post and where they come from

| Fact | Source |
|---|---|
| Both are polyethylene; HDPE density at least 0.940 g/cm³, LLDPE sheet up to 0.939 g/cm³ | GRI-GM13 (HDPE) and GRI-GM17 (LLDPE) specifications, Geosynthetic Institute |
| HDPE yields at about 12% strain; LLDPE has no yield point and breaks above 800% | GRI-GM13 / GM17 tables (minimum values) |
| HDPE needs a stress-crack test (SP-NCTL); LLDPE does not, because it is not stress-crack sensitive | GRI-GM13 / GM17 |
| LLDPE has much better multi-axial (out-of-plane) stretch than HDPE of the same thickness | Geosynthetics Society paper, "A comparison of HDPE and LLDPE geomembranes"; GRI-GM17 axi-symmetric break strain ≥ 30% |
| HDPE has higher index puncture and tear numbers at equal thickness | GRI-GM13 / GM17 tables |
| HDPE has better chemical and UV resistance; LLDPE handles settlement better | ASCE review, "Degradation of exposed LLDPE and HDPE geomembranes"; Geosynthetics Magazine (2024) |
| Leakage comes from holes and seams, not the sheet type | Giroud & Bonaparte (1989) |
| Example: 2 m dam on 10 mm gravel without geotextile, HDPE 2.0 mm vs LLDPE 1.0 mm; with geotextile both 0.75 mm | DamTech DT-ENG-003 calculator, worked example 1. The LLDPE field-puncture factor (1.5) is a DamTech engineering assumption, so the post says "in our design rules" |
| Drinking water needs a certified grade (e.g. NSF/ANSI 61) for either material | General practice. The post does not claim any certificate for a specific product |
