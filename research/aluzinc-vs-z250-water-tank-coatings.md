# Aluzinc vs Galvanised Z250 Steel for Water Storage Tanks

**Research report** · Prepared 6 November 2026 · Damtech

> **Scope.** This report compares 55% aluminium-zinc coated steel (Aluzinc, in the same generic class as Galvalume and Zincalume; "Al-Zn" or "AZ" grades below) with continuously galvanised Z250 sheet for steel water storage tanks, read against DWS 9900, AWWA D103, SANS 121 / ISO 1461, ISO 9364 and ISO 9223. It is technical decision support, not a compliance opinion or marketing copy, and it makes no statement about Damtech's own products, projects, warranties or prices. Findings rest on public sources plus the full text of DWS 9900; evidence levels stay visible throughout, because they decide how far each finding can carry.

## 1. Executive summary

**Neither bare coating should be relied on as the long-term water barrier, and Al-Zn is the stronger coating only on the freely exposed outside of a tank.** Specify the water side as a qualified lining or a heavier system (batch galvanising, duplex or a certified factory coating), and use AZ150 to AZ200 for a light-gauge shell whose water duty a lining carries. Do not put bare Al-Zn on concrete, and do not rely on it at sheared edges of panels thicker than about 2 mm. If a bare thin-sheet tank is unavoidable, zinc in proven hard, scale-forming water is the only case with a documented protective mechanism, and even that is a consumable tank rather than a long-life asset.

**The central finding is an evidence asymmetry.** The commonly quoted two-to-four-times Al-Zn advantage comes from atmospheric exposure. These searches found no fresh-water immersion rate for 55Al-Zn from any source, and that is a statement about what was found, not proof that none exists. The immersion data that did surface is seawater or salt solution, where Al-Zn tends to beat zinc but pits seriously when submerged. Atmospheric ratios must not be transferred to a tank interior, and any tank claim built on them (one South African seller advertises a [seven-fold advantage](https://www.rainbowtanks.co.za/aluzinc-vs-galvanized/) with no test cited) overreaches.

**The numbers show why the two faces need separate answers.** On the exterior, a derived planning line gives Z250 about 16, 8 and 4 years in C3, C4 and C5, against about 54, 27 and 14 years for AZ150. On the wetted face, Z250's 17.5 µm would last 3.5 to 7 years in hard water and 1.8 to 3.5 years in soft water if industry rates were sustained, which they often are not once scale forms. Al-Zn has no equivalent line. AZ150 is slightly thicker than Z250 per face but holds only about 26% of its zinc, which is why sacrificial performance, especially in low-conductivity water, cannot be assumed to follow the atmospheric ranking.

**The brief's framework needs correcting before any of this can be anchored.** DWS 9900 is a corrosion specification weighted to pipelines and names neither product. ISO 1461 / SANS 121 excludes continuously galvanised sheet. ISO 9223 is atmospheric and does not describe tank interiors. These are coil-coated sheet products, not plate. No AWWA D103 clause text was read, so everything said here about D103 is second-hand. DWS 9900 does offer a usable approval route for an unnamed system, set out in section 7. Immersion coupons in real waters, written producer positions and a purchased copy of D103 would settle most open questions (section 10).

## 3. The Al-Zn advantage is atmospheric and the immersion column is empty

### Equal thickness hides a fourfold difference in zinc

Al-Zn is a two-phase composite: aluminium-rich dendrites act as a barrier, while a connected zinc-rich interdendritic labyrinth does the sacrificial work, over a thin intermetallic layer ([GalvInfoNote 1.4](https://www.galvinfo.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/8/2017/05/GalvInfoNote_1_4.pdf)). By rule of mixtures the coating is about 75 vol% aluminium; the often-quoted 80 vol% was not traced to a primary source [Der]. Continuous galvanised sheet is close to a single near-pure zinc layer that corrodes by roughly uniform thinning. Batch galvanising differs again, with zinc-iron alloy layers usually 55 to over 100 µm thick ([HDGASA Info Sheet 6](https://www.hdgasa.org.za/wp-content/uploads/2015/12/HDGA-Info-Sheet-No6-Corrosion-Control-of-HDG-Steel-Water.pdf)), so long-life stories from batch-galvanised tanks do not transfer to sheet.

| Coating | Total g/m² (both faces) | µm per face, nominal [Der] | Zinc g/m² per face [Der] | Thinner-face estimate, µm [Der] |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Z250 | 250 | 17.5 | 125 | Not sourced; Z275's 13 to 14 µm implies about 30% below nominal |
| Z275 | 275 | 19.3 | 137.5 | 13 to 14 |
| AZ150 | 150 | 20.0 | 32.6 | 13.9 |
| AZ185 | 185 | 24.7 | 40.2 | 17.1 |
| AZ200 | 200 | 26.7 | 43.5 | 18.1 |

Thickness uses zinc at [7.14 g/cm³](https://www.galvinfo.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/8/2021/01/GalvInfoNote-1-1-Rev-4-0.pdf) and Al-Zn at 3.75 g/cm³, the density implied by the [published equivalence](https://www.galvinfo.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/8/2017/05/GalvInfoNote_1_4.pdf) of 150 g/m² with about 20 µm per face. Zinc mass assumes 43.5 wt% zinc. Thinner-face values apply the [ISO 9364 note](https://cdn.standards.iteh.ai/samples/37775/5e195748d30d42a6a6b81a1c49ffe597/ISO-9364-2006.pdf) that about 40% of the single-spot limit can be expected on either surface; the [ASTM route](https://www.galvinfo.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/8/2021/01/GalvInfoNote-1-1-Rev-4-0.pdf) gives the Z275 value. **AZ150 is about 14% thicker than Z250 and holds about 26% of its zinc; AZ200 holds about 35%.** Designations are total for both faces, so reading Z250 as a per-face figure, as batch coatings are reported, overstates each face two to one. One caution on the source: Note 3 to ISO 9364:2006 Table 6 reads ten times too thin against the density conversion in the extracted text and should not be quoted.

### Continuous immersion: zinc has rates, Al-Zn has none

Zinc in water protects sacrificially and then depends on a carbonate film that hardness and alkalinity support, so its rate varies widely with chemistry (section 4). Al-Zn's celebrated behaviour is different: interdendritic zinc corrodes first, its products plug the labyrinth, and the loss curve flattens rather than running linear ([GalvInfoNote 1.4](https://www.galvinfo.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/8/2017/05/GalvInfoNote_1_4.pdf)). That account comes from atmospheric exposure. Nothing retrieved shows the plugging operating the same way under continuous immersion.

The nearest direct electrochemistry is Vu and colleagues' current and pH mapping over cut edges: in chloride both phases activated and steel stayed protected, while in sulphate only zinc-rich phases stayed active and protection lasted only for short immersion times ([Corrosion Science 2011, extract](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0010938X11002708)). Inference: in low-chloride water the working anode is largely interdendritic zinc, about a quarter of Z250's zinc at AZ150. That is reasoning from laboratory salts, and sulphate solution is not a model for bicarbonate-bearing fresh water.

The strongest case for Al-Zn deserves a fair statement. Seawater studies report it outperforming galvanised and 5% Al-Zn overall ([Journal of Northeastern University, extract](https://xuebao.neu.edu.cn/natural/CN/Y2007/V28/I10/1425)), work using salt spray, humidity and immersion found it more resistant than zinc, particularly with chloride ([Corrosion Science 1998, extract](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0010938X98000730)), and in 0.5 mol/L NaCl its cathodic protection fell off at 260 hours against 153 hours for zinc ([record, extract](https://www.academia.edu/89610477/Corrosion_behavior_of_Zinc_or_55_Al_Zn_hot_dip_coated_steel_in_0_5_m_NaCl_solution)). Aluminium metal also resists neutral soft water well ([AERCO](https://www.aerco.com/dfsmedia/0533dbba17714b1ab581ab07a4cbb521/59138-source/technical-information-controlling-aluminum-corrosion-2-2011), closed-loop service and pure aluminium). So **Al-Zn in fresh-water immersion is unknown, not shown to be poor.** Against that, the same seawater work reports higher rates and serious pitting in the immersion zone than in tidal or splash zones ([Springer record, extract](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/BF02708631)), and chloride is precisely what a fresh-water tank lacks.

Producers do not fill the gap. BlueScope's [TB-3](https://cdn.dcs.bluescope.com.au/download/tb-03) [Prod] calls Aquaplate, a polymer-laminated product on a zinc substrate (Z450 for resin-coated walls and lids, Z275 for prepainted walls and bases), the only product it recommends for water tanks, and says of the strainer that Zincalume "must not be continually immersed". That sentence is about one component and BlueScope sells the alternative, so it needs weighing with care. Its [Corrosion Technical Bulletin CTB-16](https://www.nsbluescope.com/my/wp-content/uploads/sites/7/2020/01/CTB-16_R5_May16.pdf) probably holds the fuller position, but it returned 404 and was seen only as a search extract describing variable results for bare galvanised or Zincalume in water storage; it must be opened before being quoted. US producers corroborate from another direction: [Cleveland-Cliffs](https://d1io3yog0oux5.cloudfront.net/clevelandcliffs/files/pages/clevelandcliffs/db/1184/description/2023+Documents/CLF_Warranty_Galvalume_032023.pdf) warrants AZ50 only under normal atmospheric exposure and excludes constant fresh-water spray and failure to provide free drainage, and [U.S. Steel](https://www.stocktonproducts.com/pdf/GALVALUME_Sheet_Steel.pdf) advises against very long wet times. Zinc bodies, by contrast, [treat tanks as normal service](https://www.hdgasa.org.za/wp-content/uploads/2015/12/HDGA-Info-Sheet-No3-Corrosion-by-Water.pdf) within chemistry limits, though they promote their product and cite little primary data. Polarity reversal, in which zinc turns cathodic to steel at about 60 to 82 °C ([AGA](https://galvanizeit.org/knowledgebase/article/polarity-reversal)), is unlikely in a shaded ambient tank, though no source addresses wall temperature in small sun-heated tanks.

### Fluctuating levels: drying helps Al-Zn in the data available, none of it fresh water

A producer-authored study found 55Al-Zn corroding near shear-cut edges in continuously wet salt spray, with corrosion products adhering only when a drying step was included ([Matsumoto et al., J-STAGE, extract](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/tetsutohagane1955/91/9/91_9_700/_article/-char/en)). Zinc shows the opposite seawater pattern: tidal or splash rates are typically about twice those of fully immersed areas ([HDGASA Info Sheet 3](https://www.hdgasa.org.za/wp-content/uploads/2015/12/HDGA-Info-Sheet-No3-Corrosion-by-Water.pdf)), and the AGA adds that agitation accelerates waterline attack in fresh and sea water ([AGA](https://galvanizeit.org/knowledgebase/article/the-performance-of-hot-dip-galvanized-steel-in-water-environments)). The working hypothesis is therefore that a wall band cycling wet and dry may favour Al-Zn, while a nearly static waterline is a differential aeration site for both, worse with sediment or biofilm. No fresh-water waterline test or tank teardown with loss by height was found, so the ranking at a tank waterline remains unproven.

### High humidity: a closed headspace resembles wet storage, not a roof

Moisture trapped without air circulation gives zinc loose white rust, or dark zinc oxide stains on skin-passed sheet ([GalvInfoNote 3.3](https://www.galvinfo.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/8/2017/05/GalvInfoNote_3_3.pdf)), and it causes storage stain on Al-Zn ([U.S. Steel](https://www.stocktonproducts.com/pdf/GALVALUME_Sheet_Steel.pdf)), described as darkening of the unpainted surface ([Metl-Span, extract](https://www.metlspan.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/11/MIMP-96-Wet-Stains-Tech-Bulletin.pdf)). Mill passivation buys limited time on either ([BlueScope TB-7, extract](https://www.nsbluescope.com/my/wp-content/uploads/sites/7/2020/01/TB-7_R6_Nov03.pdf)). No tank-headspace study was found, and no measurement of coating loss from staining on either product. Inference: long wetness, soft condensate and limited air exchange make exposed-roof ratios inapplicable, and condensate lacks the hardness that protects zinc even when the stored water is hard. Humid, chemically loaded air can reverse the normal ranking, as the animal-confinement exception shows ([GalvInfoNote 1.4](https://www.galvinfo.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/8/2017/05/GalvInfoNote_1_4.pdf)). DWS 9900's closest reasoning concerns valve chambers, rated C5I for condensation (cl. 9.3.6 seq., p.90), with galvanised surfaces barred there because of humidity (cl. 7.5.1, p.56); it does not extend this to tanks.

## 4. Water chemistry swings zinc widely and leaves Al-Zn unmeasured

South African context sharpens the soft-water row. **Every Cape Town supply reported for January to March 2026 falls below HDGASA's 50 mg/L line, with hardness of 16 to 46 and alkalinity of 8 to 26 mg/L CaCO₃, pH 6.0 to 8.9 and free chlorine of 0.7 to 1.3 mg/L** ([City of Cape Town](https://www.capetown.gov.za/_documents/resource.capetown.gov.za/documentcentre/Documents/City%20research%20reports%20and%20review/Jan-March-2026-Quarterly_TapWater_Quality_Report.pdf)). An illustrative estimate for the Blackheath supply gives a Langelier index of about -1.8, strongly non-scaling [Der]. Whether Highveld municipal supply is similarly soft remains open, because no Rand Water or Vaal hardness figures were retrieved. Chlorine residual at the wall is probably lower than at the tap in a dark tank (Inference). No South African failure survey, Water Research Commission report or service-life study of zinc or Al-Zn tanks by water chemistry surfaced.

Leaching sets a separate limit on bare service. SANS 241-1:2015 lists zinc at 5 mg/L (aesthetic), aluminium at 300 µg/L (operational) and pH at 5 to 9.7 ([hosted copy](https://literature.wolkersdorfer.info/literature/Drinking%20Water%20Quality%20SANS%20241%202015%20Parts%201%202.pdf)); WHO sets [no health-based zinc value](https://cdn.who.int/media/docs/default-source/wash-documents/water-safety-and-quality/chemical-fact-sheets-2022/zinc-fact-sheet-2022.pdf?sfvrsn=890765f3_2&download=true) but notes consumer acceptability may suffer above 3 mg/L. Australian guidance reports legacy galvanised-iron rainwater tanks typically at 2 to 4 mg/L zinc and up to 11 mg/L ([NHMRC](https://guidelines.nhmrc.gov.au/australian-drinking-water-guidelines/part-5/physical-chemical-characteristics/zinc)). Field attribution is messy: in 53 Adelaide tanks, roof material related to metals while galvanised versus polyethylene tank material showed no significant effect on zinc ([Chubaka et al.](https://mdpi.com/1660-4601/15/7/1551)), so tank surveys cannot rank the two coatings. **No NSF/ANSI 61 or AS/NZS 4020 listing for a bare 55Al-Zn surface was found** (absence from these searches only), while the polymer-laminated Aquaplate is stated to meet AS/NZS 4020:2018 ([TB-3](https://cdn.dcs.bluescope.com.au/download/tb-03), [Prod]).

The pH window gives Al-Zn its one clear documented weakness. Sources place zinc's upper limit near 12 to 12.5, whereas [U.S. Steel](https://info.mcelroymetal.com/hubfs/assets/conditions-corrosive-to-hdg-and-galvalume.pdf) warns of alkali attack on both coatings above pH 9, a more conservative line than zinc bodies use and one that SANS-compliant water can exceed. Concrete is where the gap bites: GalvInfo reports galvanising embedded in sound concrete corroding below 0.1 µm/year, yet [advises no contact](https://www.galvinfo.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/8/2017/05/Galvatech-04-Presentation.pdf) between 55Al-Zn and concrete, including [cured concrete that may get wet](https://www.galvinfo.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/8/2017/05/GalvInfoNote_1_4.pdf), because alkalinity attacks the aluminium. Copper is the shared hazard, with about 0.1 ppm named for rapid pitting of zinc ([GAA](https://designmanual.gaa.com.au/index.php/durability-under-water/)), and BlueScope notes that low-pH, low-alkalinity water dissolves copper pipe, compounding the soft-water risk.

## 5. Zinc holds the larger edge reserve and Al-Zn the better marine edge

"Self-healing" oversells what either coating has been shown to do. **No immersed cut-edge or bolt-hole measurement in fresh water was found for either coating: no rate, no time to red rust and no throw distance.** What exists is atmospheric, cabinet or salt-solution data.

Zinc's edge protection comes from reserve and film. Sacrificial action at scratches can give way to barrier protection as products spread over the exposed steel ([HDGASA Info Sheet 3](https://www.hdgasa.org.za/wp-content/uploads/2015/12/HDGA-Info-Sheet-No3-Corrosion-by-Water.pdf)), and Z250 carries about 3.8 times the zinc of AZ150 per face. Cleveland-Cliffs, which sells both, says galvanised may show less cut-edge red rust than Al-Zn in the short term, under about 10 years depending on environment ([product data](https://d1io3yog0oux5.cloudfront.net/_7ce3a04c09e74bf31ff5f89fc888db25/clevelandcliffs/db/1190/10804/file/CLF_ProductData_Galvalume_03142023.pdf)). Thin sheet zinc still has limits: an interested batch-galvanising body found cut-edge protection very limited at corrosive sites, worse on thicker steel and at roll-formed corners ([GAA/GANZ, p.12](https://www.galvanizing.org.nz/docs/GAA_GANZ_Guide_to_Durability_of_HDG_1.pdf)), and heavier zinc is advised as steel thickens ([Dofasco, extract](https://dofasco.arcelormittal.com/media/t1nn0ego/amd0125-galvanize-and-galvanneal-steel-en.pdf)).

Al-Zn's edge protection is real but shallower and more conditional. Chloride activates both phases and protects the edge; in sulphate, acidifying aluminium products confine protection to short distances and do not stop red rust ([Vu et al., record, extract](https://bvbvf1.bib-bvb.de/vufind/PrimoRecord/cdi_proquest_miscellaneous_907934431)). A five-year study found both coatings giving little cathodic protection in rural and urban air and good protection in marine and industrial air, lasting longer on Al-Zn ([Palma and Morcillo, extract](https://digital.csic.es/handle/10261/76327)). Lindab's rule of thumb is that Aluzinc cannot protect a cut edge thicker than 1 to 2 mm, depending on environment and coating ([Lindab, extract, verify wording](https://cms.esi.info/Media/documents/Linda_Rainline_Aluzinc_ML.pdf)). That figure has no test basis in what was found and was not established for immersion; treating it as conservative for water would be imprudent. Inference: three indirect lines (chloride activation, the need for drying and limited galvanic throw at low conductivity) suggest Al-Zn's edge protection is weaker in fresh water than in marine air. That is a well-founded hypothesis, not a measurement.

Geometry sets the stakes. A 1 mm sheet exposes 1,000 mm² of steel per metre of edge, and 2 to 3 mm panels expose two to three times that for the same coating [Der]. Bolt holes combine a cut edge with an oxygen-depleted crevice. Forming adds a further Al-Zn caution: its intermetallic is hard and brittle, and thicker coatings crack more readily, so [U.S. Steel](https://www.ussteel.com/documents/40705/43746/Tension-Bend+Staining+of+Prepainted+GALVALUME%C2%AE+Coated+Sheet.pdf/33b319ca-6764-d906-79c5-0f5fee01521b?t=1604476483104) favours the lighter coating for tight bends, which limits the gain from moving up to AZ200. Its self-limiting account of rust-plugged cracks assumes an atmosphere that dries and should not be carried to a wet face.

**Verdict by exposure.** In atmospheric marine and industrial service over the long term, Al-Zn holds the better edge on thin sheet. In the short term, and in hard or moderately conductive water, zinc probably holds it (Inference, supported by reserve and film behaviour). In very soft water neither edge should be trusted, and in either coating, sheared edges and holes on a wetted face should be sealed or protected rather than left to galvanic action.

Siting moves these numbers more than coating grade does. South Africa's peer-reviewed map, built on mild steel, places the first 150 m in CX at 319 ± 112 µm/year, 150 to 1000 m at mid-C4 to mid-C5 at 60 ± 22 µm/year, 1 to 30 km largely in C3, and most inland areas with over 400 mm of annual rain in C2 to C3, with C4 hotspots near heavy industry ([Janse van Rensburg et al., 2019](https://sajs.co.za/article/view/4901/7962)). Those classes are by steel, and a site's zinc class need not match; no measured zinc rate for any South African site was retrieved. Sources also disagree on individual sites (SAISC reports Germiston as C5 while the paper's 51 µm/year sits just inside the C4 steel band), so classes should be measured rather than assumed. U.S. Steel also names [fly ash and coal dust](https://info.mcelroymetal.com/hubfs/assets/conditions-corrosive-to-hdg-and-galvalume.pdf) as hazards for both coatings, which matters for exteriors near Mpumalanga power stations (Inference; no South African measurement backs it).

## 7. The standards give an approval route and no product endorsement

### DWS 9900: written for galvanised articles and pipes, silent on sheet

Chapter 25 covers batch articles and tubes only: application by HDGASA members under SANS 121 / ISO 1461, or EN 10240 for tubes (cl. 25.3, p.226). Its required heavy-duty default runs at 55, 65, 80 and 105 µm for steel below 1.5 mm, 1.5 to 3 mm, 3 to 6 mm and 6 mm or more, with light-duty local minimums down to 35 µm and means to 45 µm (cl. 25.4.1.2, p.226). No coating mass in g/m² appears anywhere. Z250's 17.5 µm per face is half the lowest light-duty local minimum, 39% of the lowest light-duty mean and 32% of the lowest heavy-duty value [Der]. The table does not formally apply to sheet, but it is the only benchmark in the document and a reviewer may use it.

Four provisions bear on the decision. Galvanised structures and pipes are not to be subjected to stagnant water, on oxygen-depletion grounds, and "stagnant" is undefined (cl. 25.1, p.225). A water analysis must go to the Corrosion Engineer to decide whether an internal polymer lining or overcoat is needed (cl. 25.1, p.225), which shows that bare galvanising in water is not banned outright. Galvanised structural steel in C4 and C5 must be duplex coated, while C1 to C3 may be galvanised or duplex (cl. 25.1, p.225; cl. 7.5.1, p.56). Immersion has no Im classes; water-to-steel corrosivity uses DIN 50 929-3, a CCPP target of +2 to +5 mg/L CaCO₃ and a Corrosivity Ratio above 1 flagged as corrosive to steel (cl. 9.1.2.1, pp.79–80). None of these indices is zinc-specific or Al-Zn-specific.

Inference on status: these rules use the word "galvanised", which fits Z250 naturally and Al-Zn only by analogy. Clause 25.2(a) limits total molten-zinc impurities to 1.5% (p.225), so read literally a 55% aluminium bath is not galvanising under the chapter. That silence is not permission, and an engineer could apply the oxygen-depletion reasoning to either. Whether a tank shell counts as "structural steel" is also an interpretation. The wet or submerged duplex system is epoxy primer 40 to 80 µm, polyamine or polyamide cured epoxy 125 to 150 µm and PU of at least 50 µm, or FBE/FBPE 125 µm plus PU (cl. 25.7.2, p.229). Its surface preparation allows sweep-blasting off no more than 10 µm of zinc (cl. 25.7.1.4, p.229), more than half of Z250's face. Duplex on Z250 is therefore tight, and it also requires an unpassivated or cleaned surface (cl. 25.7.1.1–2, p.228), whereas mill sheet is commonly passivated (general knowledge, not in the specification). Fastener and joint rules apply to both coatings: compatibility or protection where potentials differ by 0.3 V (cl. 5.1.1, p.29); fasteners more noble than the base and coated or insulated (cl. 5.1.2, p.29); and for wet or submerged service stainless, fluoropolymer-coated or epoxy-overcoated options with no bare galvanised option listed (cl. 9.9.1 seq., p.99). Permanent installations in concrete point to stainless, coated and lined parts (cl. 5.1.1.3, p.29).

## 8. The trade-offs, and five limits on the argument

| Criterion | Z250 | 55Al-Zn (AZ150–AZ200) | Advantage | Evidence strength |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Exposed exterior, C3–C5 | 16 / 8 / 4 years | 54 / 27 / 14 (AZ150) | Al-Zn, about 3.4 times | Rates read for zinc; Al-Zn derived from long atmospheric programmes |
| Fresh-water immersion | Rates known, chemistry-dependent, thin reserve | No rate found | Neither provable | Zinc: trade. Al-Zn: gap |
| Hard, scale-forming water | Documented film mechanism | No scale benefit shown | Zinc | Trade, little primary data |
| Soft or acidic water | Worst case | Unknown; aluminium analogy only | Neither proven; do not rely on either | Zinc: trade. Al-Zn: gap |
| Seawater or chloride solution | Lower resistance | Better overall, but pits | Al-Zn, with pitting caveat | Abstracts |
| Cut edge, marine air, thin sheet | Short-term advantage, depletes | Longer galvanic action | Al-Zn long term | Producer and abstract |
| Cut edge, panels above about 2 mm | Weak but with more reserve | Rule of thumb says unprotected | Zinc | Producer, unverified basis |
| Immersed edge or bolt hole | No data | No data | Unknown | None |
| Forming and tight bends | Better adhesion, preferred for severe forming | Brittle layer; heavier grades crack more | Zinc | Secondary and producer |
| Concrete contact | Tolerates cured contact | Advised against, even cured if it may get wet | Zinc | Secondary, consistent |
| Closed headspace | White rust, soft condensate | Dark staining | Unknown | Storage analogy only |
| DWS 9900 position | Restrictions apply by wording; no sheet route | Unnamed, approval only | Neither | [Std] |
| Potable acceptance, bare | None found | None found | Neither | Absence from searches |

This table supports three conclusions: neither bare coating should be the water-side barrier, durability there comes from a lining or heavier system, and Al-Zn is the stronger atmospheric coating. They hold, but five parts of the supporting argument are weaker or narrower than they first look.

**The DWS 9900 leg is conditional.** The stagnant-water rule binds only under contract, names galvanised structures, leaves "stagnant" undefined and sits beside a clause that treats lining as an engineer's decision after water analysis. It supports caution rather than prohibition, and it does not reach Al-Zn except by analogy. **The Z250 arithmetic overstates the case in hard water,** because trade sources describe scale virtually stopping corrosion there, and Table B assumes a sustained rate. The honest statement is that bare zinc is a recognised service for thick batch coatings in scale-forming water (HDGASA reports one [pressed-steel tank at about 70 years](https://hdgasa.org.za/wp-content/uploads/2015/09/Steel-Protection-Guide.pdf), an anecdote with thick panels, unknown water and failed fasteners), and that 17.5 µm of sheet has no comparable record in anything found.

**The producer leg is narrower than a headline implies.** The BlueScope sentence concerns a strainer, the company sells the lined alternative, and the fuller bulletin was only seen as an extract. It stands only because US warranty scopes and U.S. Steel's guidance point the same way. Notably, the one producer-qualified tank route found uses zinc under a polymer, not Al-Zn. **The absence of Al-Zn data is not a negative result.** Chloride-bearing immersion results favour Al-Zn over zinc, and aluminium analogy leans the same way in soft water. Not relying on Al-Zn is a judgement about unproven performance on a thin coating, and it would change with data. Market use settles nothing either way: Zincalume is still [sold for tanks](https://asctanks.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/Kingspan_Water_Tanks_Rural_Brochure.pdf), and one South African maker [pairs AZ150 with a multilayer PVC liner](https://www.engineeringnews.co.za/article/sbs-water-systems-2018-11-06/rep_id:5872/company:sbs-tanks-2021-09-08) [Prod, Extract], which is consistent with the lining carrying the water duty but is not performance evidence.

**"Atmospheric side" must mean the freely exposed, draining exterior.** The inside face of a lined shell, a roof underside, the wall base with dirt against it, and any crevice are not what the exposure programmes measured. Al-Zn's advantage is also subject to the concrete, cut-edge and thin-face caveats, and to the forming limit on heavier grades. Its multiple holds best where the tank has a clean, washed outer skin on thin sheet, which is the configuration recommended below.

## 9. Engineering recommendation by tank configuration

**Plain answer: for wet-environment tank construction, specify the water barrier first and the metallic coating second. On that basis, Al-Zn is the preferred coating for a lined light-gauge shell, and zinc is the preferred family wherever metal must meet water or concrete directly, but in batch or heavier form rather than as Z250.** Bare Z250 is the default choice for no configuration here. It remains a low-cost option only for short-life or low-corrosivity duty where the owner accepts that life explicitly.

|---|---|---|---|---|

AZ or Z250 sheet only via section 7's route | Unqualified claims of DWS compliance for sheet | Chapter 25 offers no sheet route | High on route; tank scope is Inference |

Across all configurations, specify the coating per face, or require measured values on the wet face, since either face may be about 30% below nominal; confirm the alloy on the mill certificate (AZ versus magnesium-bearing grades); seal cut edges and holes; isolate dissimilar metals and select fasteners with matching life; and keep dirt and vegetation off the wall base. At around 0.5 mm base metal the margin after coating loss is short, although steel loss rates after coating consumption were not sourced.

## 11. Conclusion

The useful shift is from asking which coating is better to asking which face of the tank a claim describes. The Al-Zn reputation is genuine and well earned on outdoor sheet, and the error lies in moving it indoors and under water, where the same two-phase structure holds only about a quarter of the sacrificial zinc and has no published fresh-water record in what was found. Zinc's position is almost the mirror image: weaker in air and thinner in Z250 form, but the only coating here with a described mechanism for protecting itself in water, and only when the water cooperates.

For a specifier, this makes the lining or heavier system the real durability decision and turns the metallic coating into a secondary, exterior one, where Al-Zn usually wins. The most direct way to retire this uncertainty is also practical: two years of coupons in the waters a tank will actually hold would produce evidence that these searches could not find, and it would feed straight into DWS 9900's comparative testing route.
